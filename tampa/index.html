Not every leak is an emergency, but some are, and it helps to know the difference. What to Do Before Our Tampa Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Water from a broken supply line or an overflowing sink is the safest kind. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Discharge from appliances, such as a dishwasher or washer, carries more bacteria, and gloves and hand washing are sensible. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

In every case, there are simple ways to save the crew time when it reaches Tampa. Make room for the truck near the entrance and clear the route from the front door to the damage. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Have your insurance details at hand if you can reach them without trouble.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. So even for a leak in Tampa that looks minor, the first hour is the one to use well.

Restoration Challenges Tampa Homeowners Face

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Tampa, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. Our Tampa technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Tampa.
